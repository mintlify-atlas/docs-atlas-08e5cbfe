Dawson's Reef sits just southeast of Cooktown in Far North Queensland, close enough to shore to make it an accessible entry point into the broader Great Barrier Reef region. Identified on marine-park maps as reef 15-051, it lies near the Annan and Endeavour river systems, and Cooktown serves as the most practical base for reaching the area by boat. The reef draws local operators primarily for reef fishing, and the Australian Institute of Marine Science actively monitors water quality at the site.

## Location and Marine Park Context

Dawson's Reef is within the Great Barrier Reef Marine Park, near the confluence of the Annan and Endeavour river systems southeast of Cooktown in Far North Queensland. Its designation as reef 15-051 on marine-park maps means zoning rules — which govern what activities are permitted and where — apply directly to the site. Cooktown is the gateway town for organizing boat access to this part of the reef system.

<Info>
  The Australian Institute of Marine Science (AIMS) monitors water quality at Dawson's Reef, tracking indicators such as turbidity and chlorophyll levels as part of broader Great Barrier Reef health assessments. Data is accessible via the [AIMS metadata catalogue](https://apps.aims.gov.au/metadata/view/8a698de1-3fbf-48a5-b068-358b07aad35c).
</Info>

## Planning Your Trip

<Steps>
  <Step title="Use Cooktown as your base">
    Cooktown is the closest practical starting point for boat access to Dawson's Reef. Local operators depart from here and know the specific navigation routes, tides, and conditions for the area.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Book with a Cooktown operator">
    Choose an operator with direct experience at reef 15-051. Ask about current zoning rules, what activities are permitted, and whether the reef is accessible on the planned date.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Understand current fishing rules">
    Reef fishing in the Great Barrier Reef Marine Park is governed by zoning and species-specific regulations. Confirm bag limits, size limits, and any temporary closures with your operator before departure.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check weather and reef-protection requirements">
    Far North Queensland weather can change quickly, especially during the wet season. Your operator will advise on safe weather windows and on any reef-protection requirements that apply to the current zoning at reef 15-051.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  All visits to Dawson's Reef should be arranged through a Cooktown-based operator who understands current marine-park zoning, weather patterns, fishing rules, and reef-protection requirements. Zoning rules in the Great Barrier Reef Marine Park can change — always verify the current status with your operator rather than relying on outdated maps or guides.
</Note>

<Accordion title="What does AIMS monitoring tell us about Dawson's Reef?">
  The Australian Institute of Marine Science tracks water-quality indicators including turbidity (water clarity) and chlorophyll levels at Dawson's Reef. Turbidity affects visibility and light penetration for coral growth; elevated chlorophyll levels can indicate nutrient runoff from nearby river systems such as the Annan and Endeavour. This data forms part of AIMS' long-term Great Barrier Reef health monitoring program.
</Accordion>

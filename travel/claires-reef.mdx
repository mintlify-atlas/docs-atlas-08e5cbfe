Claire's Reef is a lagoonal patch reef nestled within Bermuda's North Lagoon at approximately 32.4094° north and 64.8410° west. The top of the reef rises to roughly three meters below the surface, making it one of the shallower sites within Bermuda's extensive network of northern reefs. The surrounding waters are characteristically clear, and the reef supports a diverse community of hard corals, reef fish, sponges, mollusks, crustaceans, and other marine life typical of Bermuda's lagoonal environment.

## Location and Depth

Claire's Reef sits in Bermuda's North Lagoon, a broad expanse of relatively sheltered water on the northern side of the island. Its coordinates — approximately 32.4094° N, 64.8410° W — place it within the lagoonal patch reef network that makes Bermuda one of the northernmost coral reef systems in the Atlantic. At around three meters to the reef top, it is accessible to snorkelers as well as divers, depending on conditions on the day.

<Info>
  Claire's Reef is documented in Bermuda's State of the Environment report, which covers the island's marine ecosystems including the North Lagoon reef network. The report is available from the [Department of Planning, Government of Bermuda](https://planning.gov.bm/wp-content/uploads/2018/11/State-of-the-Environment.pdf).
</Info>

## How to Visit

A locally guided boat excursion or snorkeling trip is the best way to explore Bermuda's North Lagoon and reach Claire's Reef. Local operators know which sites are accessible on any given day based on visibility, weather, and sea state — factors that vary and affect both safety and the quality of the experience.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Book a guided boat or snorkeling excursion">
    Contact a Bermuda-based operator who runs North Lagoon tours. They will know the current condition of the reef, how to reach it safely, and whether it is accessible on your planned date.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Ask about Claire's Reef specifically">
    When booking, ask the operator whether access to Claire's Reef is included or available on the itinerary. Operators may visit multiple patch reef sites within the North Lagoon and can advise which are most suitable.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm conditions before departing">
    Check visibility, weather, and sea state with your operator on the morning of the trip. Conditions in the North Lagoon can change, and operators make final access decisions based on real-time information.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Follow reef-safe practices throughout">
    Avoid touching or standing on the coral, use reef-safe sunscreen, and do not collect shells, coral, or any marine life. Bermuda's reefs are protected under national legislation.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Before departing, ask your operator directly about current visibility, expected weather, and which reef-safe practices they require on the excursion. Good operators will brief you on these points before you reach the water — if they don't, it's worth asking.
</Tip>

<Accordion title="What makes Bermuda's North Lagoon reefs distinctive?">
  Bermuda sits at roughly 32° north latitude, making it one of the northernmost coral reef systems in the world. The warm waters of the Gulf Stream keep sea temperatures high enough to support coral growth. The North Lagoon's relatively sheltered, shallow waters create conditions where lagoonal patch reefs like Claire's Reef can thrive, supporting biodiversity that would not typically be found this far north in the Atlantic.
</Accordion>

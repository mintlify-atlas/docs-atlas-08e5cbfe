A safe beach day begins before anyone steps onto the sand. Checking conditions, knowing where to swim, and preparing the group for changing weather are decisions that take minutes but can make a meaningful difference. The guidance on this page draws from the National Park Service and National Weather Service.

## Plan a Safer Beach Day

Before heading out, gather information about the specific beach you plan to visit. A sunny forecast does not guarantee calm water, and conditions at one beach can differ from another just a short drive away.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Check conditions before you leave">
    Look up the local weather forecast, surf reports, rip-current statements, water-quality notices, and any active beach advisories. Review posted beach flags and confirm lifeguard hours for the day you plan to visit.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose a swimming area that fits your group">
    Select a lifeguarded beach when possible. Identify the guarded swimming zone when you arrive, ask about any hazards you do not recognize, and keep non-swimmers away from drop-offs and rough surf.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Swim with a buddy and supervise children">
    The National Park Service advises all visitors to swim with a buddy. Keep children under active adult supervision at all times — a lifeguard on duty does not replace direct personal attention.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pack the right supplies">
    Bring drinking water, sunscreen, protective clothing, a brimmed hat, sunglasses, and footwear suitable for hot sand or sharp surfaces. Include any personal medications.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Establish a meeting point">
    Before the group spreads out, agree on a clear landmark as a meeting point in case anyone becomes separated.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Be ready to change the plan">
    If warnings are posted, waves are stronger than expected, or someone is uncomfortable, choose a walk, indoor activity, or a different beach instead. Safe planning means noticing changing conditions early enough to make a better decision.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Warning>
  **Leave the beach immediately when you hear thunder.** The National Weather Service states that anyone who can hear thunder is close enough to be struck by lightning. Move to a substantial building or hard-topped vehicle and wait at least 30 minutes after the last sound of thunder before returning to the beach.
</Warning>

## Understanding Rip Currents

A rip current is a concentrated flow of water moving away from the shoreline through the surf zone. It can carry a swimmer away from the beach — including someone who is physically strong. Rip-current risk changes with waves, sandbars, weather, and coastal structures, which is why local forecasts and lifeguard instructions matter every visit.

### Before You Enter the Water

Look for beach flags and posted safety signs before swimming. Ask a lifeguard where hazards are present and swim only in the guarded area. The National Park Service recommends avoiding the water entirely when rip-current warnings or dangerous surf conditions are posted. Do not assume that calm-looking gaps between breaking waves are automatically safer — those gaps are often where rip currents form.

<Info>
  **If you are caught in a rip current:**

  1. **Stay calm.** Do not exhaust yourself fighting the current head-on.
  2. **Swim parallel to shore** to move out of the narrow channel of the current.
  3. **Return to the beach at an angle** once you have moved beyond the current.
  4. **If you cannot escape**, float or tread water, conserve energy, and signal for help by waving your arm.
  5. **Follow local emergency guidance** — shoreline shapes and rescue procedures vary by beach.
</Info>

### If Someone Else Is in Trouble

Do not enter the water without proper training and flotation equipment. Entering the water unprepared can create a second person who needs rescue.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Alert a Lifeguard" icon="whistle" href="https://www.nps.gov/caco/planyourvisit/beachsafe.htm">
    Signal for help immediately and keep the person in your sight at all times while waiting for trained responders.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Call Emergency Services" icon="phone" href="https://www.nps.gov/caco/planyourvisit/beachsafe.htm">
    If no lifeguard is present, call emergency services right away. Provide your location, the number of people in difficulty, and any landmarks nearby.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Use a Flotation Device" icon="life-ring" href="https://www.nps.gov/caco/planyourvisit/beachsafe.htm">
    Throw or extend a flotation device if one is available. Do not enter the water yourself unless you are a trained rescuer with flotation equipment.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Follow Responder Instructions" icon="bullhorn" href="https://www.nps.gov/calo/planyourvisit/swimming-safety.htm">
    Keep the person in view and follow the instructions of lifeguards or emergency services as they arrive.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>
